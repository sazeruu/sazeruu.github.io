ATS-friendly resume checklist: 10 things to check

Updated 2026-10-12 · Free guide by Sazeru Analytics

Many companies use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to store and search resumes. It does not make the final decision, but a resume that the software cannot read properly may never be seen by a person. Use this checklist before you apply.

What is an ATS?

An ATS is software that collects resumes from a job portal, stores them and lets recruiters search by keywords such as skills, job titles and location. A recruiter then reads the resumes that look relevant. So your resume must be easy for software to read and for a person to scan.

The checklist

1. Use a simple, one-column layout

Avoid tables, text boxes, two-column designs and graphics for important information. Software may read them in the wrong order.

2. Use standard section headings

Use clear headings like Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Projects. Creative headings such as "My Journey" can confuse the software.

3. Match the words in the job post

If the job asks for "Power BI" and you know it, write "Power BI", not just "dashboard tools". Use the same wording, but only for skills you really have.

4. Write acronyms in full once

For example, "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)". Recruiters may search for either form.

5. Keep contact details in the main body

Some systems skip text in headers and footers. Put your name, phone and email at the top of the page body.

6. Use a normal font and clear formatting

Fonts like Arial, Calibri or Helvetica are safe. Use normal bullet points and avoid icons or symbols in place of words.

7. Choose the right file type

A text-based PDF works with most modern systems. If the portal asks for a Word file (DOCX), use that. Never send a photo or scan of your resume, because the software cannot read the text in an image.

8. Be consistent with dates

Use one style throughout, such as "Jan 2026 - Jun 2026".

9. Name the file clearly

Use something like Yourname_Role_Resume.pdf, not "final_final2.pdf".

10. Check spelling and keep it to one or two pages

A misspelt skill may not match a search. One page is enough for freshers, and two pages at most for experienced candidates.

Good to know: No tool can promise a resume will "beat" every ATS, because each company configures its own system. A clean format with the right keywords gives you the best chance.

Quick final check

  • One column, no tables or graphics for key details
  • Standard headings
  • Keywords from the job post, for skills you really have
  • Contact details in the body, not the header
  • Text-based PDF (or DOCX if requested)
  • Clear file name
  • No spelling mistakes

Frequently asked questions

Is a PDF or a Word file better for ATS?

Text-based PDFs work with most systems today. If the job portal or the job post asks for DOCX, follow that instruction.

Can I use a colourful resume template?

You can use light colour for headings, but avoid heavy graphics, columns and icons. Plain and clean is safer.

How many keywords should I add?

Add the ones that truly match your skills and the job post, placed naturally in your skills section and bullet points. Do not copy the whole job post.
